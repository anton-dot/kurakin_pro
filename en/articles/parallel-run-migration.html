Parallel Run: Compare Two Systems Without Charging Twice

Most successful comparisons match, but what happened to the commissions that produced no comparison at all? They matter before the cutover.

The finance team is replacing a partner commission engine. The new implementation is easier to maintain and performs well in testing. It produces almost the same amounts as the old one. Unfortunately, the legacy application contains special contracts, date-based exceptions and rounding conventions that few people fully understand.

Switching actual payments to the replacement overnight is risky. Parallel Run lets both implementations calculate on comparable inputs for a period, so differences can be investigated before the final cutover. Done poorly, however, it can create a second payment or give the company misplaced confidence.

Run the calculation twice, not the payment

Separate calculating a commission from executing the payout. The existing system remains the only authority to create a payable obligation and instruct the bank. The replacement receives the necessary inputs and calculates its expected result without sending payments, notifications or writes to the live account balance.

This is often called a shadow run. The important part is the boundary, not the label. The candidate should not accidentally possess real payment permissions. Limit external calls and destinations technically instead of relying on a rule written in the team's documentation. If the calculation currently changes business state, isolate that effect or replay the logic in a suitable test environment.

Give each comparison a stable operation identifier linking its input, rule versions, both outputs and any missing result. That becomes especially important after a worker restarts or a message is delivered again.

Match the inputs before judging the outputs

Two correct algorithms may return different amounts if they read different exchange rates, contract versions, effective dates or refunds. Use a consistent input snapshot where possible, or explicitly record which data versions were read. Otherwise the team may fix code that merely observed a different state.

Money requires clear agreements on units, rounding, discount order and negative adjustments. A small difference may be an intended new contractual rule, or it may repeat across a large volume of work. Absolute difference alone is not a sufficient acceptance criterion.

The older engine is not the definition of truth merely because it is older. If the replacement reveals a long-standing mistake, the owner of financial policy must decide what the correct treatment is. Fixing a legacy bug is a separate business decision, not automatically a failed comparison.

No comparison is itself an important observation

Suppose a report says almost all successfully paired calculations match. Some candidate calculations timed out, some inputs could not be transformed, and other events never reached the shadow processor. A percentage calculated only from successful pairs ignores precisely the work that failed to produce evidence.

Track at least four outcomes: matches, explained differences, unexplained differences and operations without a valid pair. That last group includes missing, failed and expired comparisons. Report them against all operations admitted to the trial, not only the ones that finished cleanly.

Exercise the lifecycle too: refunds after payout, retrospective corrections, cancellations and repeated operations. A convenient batch of standard agreements does not prove that rare but important adjustments behave correctly.

Who decides whether a difference is acceptable?

Every category needs an owner. Engineering can explain which rule version executed. Finance must decide whether the outcome matches the contract. If the business changes a rule during the trial, preserve its version and effective date rather than rewriting the meaning of older results after the fact.

Keep enough diagnostic information to investigate without copying personal and payment data into unrestricted comparison logs. Operation identity, rule versions and appropriately protected amounts are usually more useful than a full dump of every connected system.

Two clean days do not guarantee adequate coverage. Some partner commissions are calculated monthly, while corrections may be rarer. Know which critical scenarios were actually observed in the trial and which were tested separately with prepared examples.

Cutover needs its own evidence

Before switching authority, agree on acceptable differences, the treatment of critical unexplained cases, the ownership of unfinished work and how actual payouts will be monitored. The replacement is about to execute real actions for the first time. Check its permissions and idempotent payment handling; matching shadow calculations did not prove either.

Rolling back may also have a boundary. The new engine may have created financial state that the old one cannot interpret. Decide in advance when correction forward or manual reconciliation is required. Amazon Builders' Library explains why data compatibility limits safe rollback.

The Parallel Run is finished when the replacement correctly calculates and pays, missing cases are accounted for, differences are explainable and the older engine has actually stopped executing. Until then, supporting both systems is a deliberate cost of reducing migration risk.
