Skills Matter More Than Job Titles

A title is convenient for an org chart, but it is becoming worse at describing a person's real value — especially when technology keeps changing the content of work.

We are used to building careers through role names: specialist, senior, lead, manager, director. That is convenient for HR systems and org charts, but it increasingly fails to describe what someone can actually do.

The same title can mean completely different responsibility in two companies. And one employee may already have skills far beyond the formal job description.

A title is a container, not a capability

A role name gives quick context, but it does not answer the most useful question: what problems can this person solve independently?

For the business, it is often more valuable to know whether someone can negotiate, design a system, lead change, build a team, or work with data than to know the current title alone.

AI accelerates the ageing of familiar roles

When part of a job is automated, the role does not always disappear. More often its content changes.

People spend less time drafting, searching, or processing routine work and more time framing the problem, checking outputs, adding context, and making decisions.

If the career model is tied only to old responsibilities, it starts lagging behind actual work.

Skills-first does not mean eliminating roles

Organizations still need clear responsibility, structure, and roles.

But inside that structure it helps to see skills separately: which already exist, which are critical for the next stage, and where the company depends on only a few people.

It changes hiring

Instead of searching for someone with the perfect title, companies can look for the combination of skills and scale of experience they actually need.

A strong candidate may not have followed the expected career ladder but may already have solved problems of the right complexity.

It changes development

Not every strong specialist should become a manager in order to grow.

If a company can recognize growth in expertise, influence, and problem complexity separately from headcount responsibility, people gain more ways to develop.

It changes internal mobility

When skills are visible, it becomes easier to notice people who can move into an adjacent function or project without starting a completely new profession.

The company gains more ways to fill needs internally, and employees feel less pressure to leave in order to grow.

The career ladder becomes a career map

I would not remove job titles. I would stop treating them as the main description of a person.

In a fast-changing environment, it is more useful to know not only “what is this person's role?” but also “what can they do, at what level of complexity, and where can they grow next?”
