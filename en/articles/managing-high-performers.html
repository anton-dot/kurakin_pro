How to Manage High Performers

High performers do not need constant control, but that does not mean they need no management. They especially need meaningful scope, clear expectations, and honest feedback.

High performers can create the illusion that a manager does not need to spend time on them: work gets done, problems get solved, results appear.

That is one reason strong employees are sometimes lost unexpectedly.

Do not reward strength with more work

It is easy to give every difficult problem to the reliable person. Eventually they receive not more opportunity, but simply more of everyone else's problems.

If strong performance always leads to extra load, people quickly learn the price of success.

Give scope, not only difficulty

Strong employees need more than an endless sequence of hard tasks. They need broader context, ownership of outcomes, and the ability to improve the system.

That does not automatically mean becoming a manager.

Do not be afraid to expect more

Managers sometimes avoid difficult feedback with stars because they fear losing them.

But strong people also need to know when their behavior hurts the team, when quality is not enough, and when expectations have changed.

Do not let them become irreplaceable

An employee who alone understands a critical system or owns a key process may look extremely valuable. They are also an organizational risk.

Real strength is not only what a person can do personally, but what remains around them: knowledge, processes, and stronger colleagues.

Discuss the future before an offer appears

Development should not become a topic only after an employee announces they are leaving. By that point, the decision is often already made.

High performers are not retained by a lack of pressure. They stay where they continue to grow, influence outcomes, and do work worthy of their level.
