Publication planning software should plan delivery — not corrupt the manuscript structure.

A book has chapters. A publication process has stages, deadlines, milestones, checklists, briefs and decisions. They are connected, but they are not the same structure.

The useful separation

Keep the manuscript tree as the source of truth for the text. Keep the publication plan as the source of truth for what must happen around the text.

Why ordinary task lists are not enough

A generic task manager can remind you to finish proofreading. What it usually does not know is which book, article, section brief, editorial stage or publishing package that task belongs to. Writers then maintain the manuscript in one app and the delivery process somewhere else.

B-Maker keeps the publication plan beside the writing project: stages can have dates, status, milestones, checklist items and notes, while the manuscript itself remains a manuscript.

Article and book wizards solve a different problem

A publication plan answers when and what happens next. A writing brief answers what this piece is trying to become. For articles, that can include the main thesis, reader outcome, audience, tone, channel, editorial requirements, source material and section plan. A book can use a higher-level outline and publication stages without forcing every chapter into a task card.

This is especially useful for recurring publication: an article can begin as a brief, become manuscript, move through review, and later remain part of a larger book project.

What to look for in publication planning software

The useful questions are practical: Can the plan stay connected to the manuscript? Can stages exist without becoming chapters? Can you see upcoming and overdue work? Can article briefs and book planning coexist? Can a finished plan lead into preview, publishing package and export without another migration?

B-Maker is deliberately not trying to replace Jira or a team PM suite. The point is to cover the author-sized planning layer that sits between “I am writing” and “this is ready to publish.”
