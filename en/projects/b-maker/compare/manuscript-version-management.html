RevisionVersions

Manuscript version management without final-final-really-final.docx.

Revision should make the text safer to change, not create a maze of duplicated files. The useful unit of history is often a chapter, scene, or article — not a copy of the entire project.

Why whole-project copies become noisy

Duplicating a complete manuscript before every serious rewrite feels safe, but it quickly creates ambiguity. Which copy contains the newest Chapter 3? Which one has the editor’s comment? Did you change the synopsis in the old folder or the new one? Backups protect against loss; they are not always the best interface for creative revision.

Version the part that is changing

If Chapter 7 is being rewritten, preserve Chapter 7. If an article needs a major update, keep the published version and create a new working version. This keeps history close to the text that generated it and avoids multiplying unrelated project state.

Use names that explain intent

Version 2 is less useful than Before structural edit, Editor pass, or Published 2026-08. A version name should tell future you why the snapshot exists.

Lock the versions that represent a decision

An approved, submitted, or published version should be harder to change accidentally. Read-only locking makes the boundary visible while still allowing the text to be inspected or compared.

Compare before you copy old wording back

Returning to an older idea should not mean opening two windows and scanning paragraphs manually. A dedicated comparison view makes inserted, removed, and changed material easier to reason about before deciding what belongs in the current version.

Keep comments attached to context

Editorial feedback is most useful when it remains attached to the version and passage it refers to. Otherwise a note like “this paragraph is unclear” becomes meaningless after the paragraph moves or is rewritten.

Backups still matter

Version history and backups solve different problems. Versions help you reason about deliberate creative change. Backups protect the project against accidental loss or corruption. A serious writing workflow benefits from both.

How B-Maker handles this

Chapters, scenes, and articles can keep multiple named versions. You can duplicate, choose the current version, lock finished versions, compare revisions, and keep comments at whole-text or anchored-passage level. The project can also maintain manual backups separately.
