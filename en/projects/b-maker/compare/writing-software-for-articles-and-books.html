ArticlesBooks

Writing software for articles and books in one project.

You do not have to choose between an article archive and a book-writing app. Start with shorter pieces, move published work out of the way, and let a larger manuscript grow from the same material.

The short version

B-Maker can hold articles, essays, research notes, and a book structure in the same portable project. Drafts stay visible, published pieces can move into an organizational folder excluded from book export, and every section remains searchable, versioned, editable, and separately exportable.

Most books do not begin with “Chapter 1”

A long-form project often begins as fragments: one article that deserves a follow-up, three notes around the same idea, a conference talk, a research file, or a series of posts that gradually reveal a bigger argument. A normal word processor handles each file well, but the archive becomes harder to manage as the number of pieces grows.

B-Maker treats the archive itself as a writing project. You can begin with one article and introduce deeper structure only when the material needs it.

A practical article workflow

A project can start with folders such as Drafts, Published, Research, and Book draft. Active pieces stay near the top. When an article is published, move it into Published and collapse that branch. The text remains searchable and editable, but completed work stops occupying attention every time the project opens.

Organizational folders can be excluded from book export together with their subtree. Your archive can therefore live beside the future manuscript without accidentally becoming part of the final EPUB or PDF.

Versions matter for articles too

Articles are rarely finished in one pass. B-Maker keeps multiple revisions inside the same section, so the workflow does not require files named article-final.docx, article-final-2.docx, and article-really-final.docx. Compare revisions, lock a finished version, and return to an older direction later.

From article series to book manuscript

When related pieces accumulate, the archive does not need to be rebuilt. A series can become a manual collection, a saved search, or a branch in the manuscript tree. Notes on the Idea Map can become real chapters. Existing sections can be reordered into a larger structure while the original published pieces stay archived.

This workflow is especially useful for nonfiction authors, technical writers, consultants, researchers, and anyone whose future book is likely to emerge from material written over months or years.

Export one article today, a book later

B-Maker can export a selected section with its nested subsections instead of the whole project. The same project can produce one DOCX article today and a complete EPUB later.

When a simple folder is still enough

If every article is independent and you never need versions, shared research, planning, relationships, or a growing long-form structure, ordinary files may remain the simpler solution. B-Maker becomes more useful when the writing starts forming a body of work rather than a pile of unrelated documents.

Start with one article.

Do not build a publishing system before you have something to publish. Open B-Maker, write one piece, and let the project become more structured only when your writing does.
