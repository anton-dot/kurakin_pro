How to turn articles into a book without pretending they were chapters all along.

A pile of good articles is valuable raw material, but a book needs a new structure, fewer repetitions, stronger transitions, and a clear promise to the reader. The useful shortcut is preserving the material — not skipping the rewrite.

1. Keep the original articles intact

Do not begin by editing your published archive destructively. Preserve the published version so you can still see what readers saw, then create working versions for the book. This gives you freedom to cut, merge, and rewrite without losing the source.

2. Group by argument, not publication date

The order in which you published articles is rarely the order a book should use. Group material around reader questions, themes, stages, or a narrative progression. A temporary collection can help test an order before you change the actual manuscript structure.

3. Find overlap before writing transitions

Repeated introductions, definitions, anecdotes, and conclusions are normal in standalone articles because every article had to make sense by itself. In a book those repetitions feel padded. Identify them first; then decide which version becomes canonical and which sections can assume prior context.

4. Build chapters from material, not from filenames

One article may become half a chapter. Three short articles may become one chapter. A useful paragraph may move into a completely different section. Treat the article as source material, not a container you must preserve.

5. Add the missing connective tissue

A book needs a beginning, progression, and destination. Once the raw material is rearranged, write the parts that never needed to exist online: transitions, framing chapters, summaries, exercises, references, or a larger argument connecting the pieces.

6. Keep published articles archived inside the same project

An organizational folder such as Published can keep the originals searchable while remaining excluded from the book export. Your manuscript and source archive stay together without the archive appearing in the final book.

7. Prepare the result as a book

When the manuscript is coherent, switch from article workflow to book workflow: full-manuscript reading, analytics, editorial checks, cover and illustrations, publishing metadata, readiness checklist, and export to EPUB, PDF, or DOCX.

How B-Maker supports the transition

Articles, archived published versions, collections, manuscript sections, versions, notes, analytics, and publishing preparation can stay inside one .bmaker project. You can begin as an article writer and let the project become a book when the material earns it.
