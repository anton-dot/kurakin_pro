B-Maker vs Plottr

Plottr specializes in visual outlining and story bibles. B-Maker now has a much deeper planning layer too, but its main difference remains continuity: map ideas, plan the story, write the manuscript, revise it, analyze it, and prepare publication inside the same local-first project.

Short answer

Choose Plottr if timelines, templates, series planning, and a mature specialist story-planning workflow are the center of your process. Try B-Maker if you want visual planning to stay tied directly to the manuscript and continue through revisions, editorial checks, analytics, and publishing.

Plottr is still the specialist

Plottr is built around visual planning. Its timeline, scene cards, plotlines, story bibles, characters, places, tags, templates, filters, and series views are designed around the plotting stage. For writers who architect novels before drafting, that specialization remains a real advantage.

Plottr also has a Pro offering with web access, cloud storage, automatic synchronization, offline support and live collaboration. B-Maker does not currently offer an equivalent backend service.

B-Maker's planning layer is no longer just a story grid

B-Maker now includes a freeform multi-board Idea Map with real project entities, search, filters, minimap, images, auto layout, grouping, stacks, magnetic areas, and JSON import/export. Chapters, scenes, characters and plotlines can be dragged onto boards, and map notes or groups can become manuscript sections with an initial text version.

Alongside that, B-Maker has a manuscript board with statuses and filters, character relationships and arcs, plotlines, locations, and a story grid. Its visual planning is therefore substantially deeper than the earlier version of this comparison described.

The main difference is still the handoff

Plottr's product identity remains specialist planning. B-Maker's main idea is that planning does not have to end with an export into another writing program. The same entity can exist in the map, planning views, and manuscript, and the project continues into versions, comments, checks, analytics, cover/illustrations, and publishing preparation.

That makes B-Maker broader. Plottr remains stronger where specialization matters most: timeline depth, plotting templates, series-level planning, and connected cloud collaboration.

Local-first versus connected cloud workflow

B-Maker is local-first. A project is a portable .bmaker file, and the web app can download the browser project as that file. Plottr supports desktop use and, with Plottr Pro, a connected workflow across web, desktop and mobile with cloud synchronization.

Neither model is universally better. Some writers prioritize control and a portable file; others value automatic synchronization and collaboration more.

When Plottr makes more sense

  • Visual timelines are central to how you build a story.
  • You want established plotting templates.
  • You are planning a multi-book series.
  • You want cloud sync and collaboration through Plottr Pro.
  • You are comfortable drafting in another writing tool after planning.

When B-Maker may fit better

  • You want map, planning entities and actual manuscript in the same project.
  • You want freeform map notes to become real manuscript sections.
  • You want revisions, text checks, analytics, illustrations and publishing output after drafting.
  • You prefer local-first project ownership.
  • You also organize article series or working archives alongside books.

Verdict

Plottr remains the stronger dedicated plotting product. B-Maker's advantage is continuity rather than deeper specialization: the same project can move from visual idea to manuscript to revision to publishing without a planned handoff into another writing application.
