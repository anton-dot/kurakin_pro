B-Maker vs Scapple

Scapple is deliberately simple freeform thinking space. B-Maker's Idea Map lives inside the writing project and can work with real chapters, characters, plotlines, and planning notes.

B-Maker / Guides & comparisons / B-Maker vs Scapple
What this page is about

Choose Scapple when you want a focused visual scratchpad with minimal structure. Choose B-Maker when the map should stay connected to the manuscript and eventually turn ideas into real sections.

Two different jobs

Scapple is built as virtual paper: put notes anywhere, connect them, group them, and think without being forced into a hierarchy. That lack of project semantics is a feature when you want pure exploration.

B-Maker also allows freeform boards, but its map can contain real project entities. A character, chapter, scene, plotline, image, or planning card can appear on the board without becoming a disconnected copy.

When ideas become manuscript

In B-Maker, notes and groups on the Idea Map can become real manuscript sections with an initial text version. You can also move between the map and editor and add existing structure branches to a board.

That continuity matters if your visual thinking is not the final destination. It removes the manual step of recreating an outline or copying cards into the writing application.

Where Scapple remains stronger

Scapple is a mature specialist tool with a very clear purpose and native desktop workflow on macOS and Windows. If all you need is a lightweight canvas beside another writing app, its simplicity is hard to beat.

B-Maker makes more sense when you want the map to be one view of a larger writing system rather than a separate file.

Frequently asked questions

Can B-Maker Idea Map work as a freeform board?

Yes. It supports free positioning, connections, groups, images, multiple boards, search, a minimap, and auto-layout.

What makes B-Maker Idea Map different from a normal mind map?

Nodes can be actual manuscript and planning entities, and planning notes can become real sections.
