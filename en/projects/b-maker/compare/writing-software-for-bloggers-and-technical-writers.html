ArticlesTechnical writing

Your article archive should become an asset, not a graveyard.

Bloggers and technical writers often produce dozens or hundreds of pieces over time. The problem is not creating one more document — it is keeping drafts, published work, revisions, research, and reusable material under control.

A project for a body of work

B-Maker can hold active drafts, published articles, notes, versions, collections, saved searches, and longer manuscripts together. Published material can stay searchable without cluttering the active writing view.

Drafts and published work can live together

Create an organizational folder such as Published and move finished pieces there. The folder can stay excluded from book export, so archived articles do not accidentally appear in a manuscript. Collapse it and the working tree stays clean.

Use collections for editorial workflows

A structural folder says where an article belongs. A collection can say what you are doing with it. Build a manual collection for this month’s editing queue, or a saved search for drafts that still contain a particular marker. The content does not need to move just because the workflow changes.

Versions matter for technical content

Technical articles age. Commands change, APIs move, screenshots become obsolete, and explanations improve. Multiple versions let you preserve a published state, create a new revision, compare changes, and lock a version that represents what readers actually saw.

Markdown without giving up a visual editor

B-Maker’s project model keeps portable Markdown while the editor presents formatted content. Import TXT, Markdown, or DOCX and export a whole branch or an individual article to formats including Markdown, TXT, DOCX, PDF, and EPUB where appropriate.

Articles can become something larger

A long-running series may eventually become a handbook, course companion, essay collection, or book. Because the articles, notes, versions, and research are already together, the transition begins with reorganizing existing work rather than rebuilding context from scratch.

Good fit if…

You write repeatedly on related subjects and want your archive, drafts, and long-form projects to reinforce each other instead of living as isolated files in folders.
