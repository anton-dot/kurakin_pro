NonfictionResearch + structure

Nonfiction writing software for projects that keep accumulating context.

Nonfiction rarely begins as a clean book. It begins as notes, articles, research, drafts, examples, discarded sections, and arguments that slowly become a manuscript.

One place for the working material and the book

B-Maker lets research, notes, article drafts, organizational folders, manuscript sections, versions, analytics, and publishing metadata live inside the same portable project.

Start before you know the final structure

You can begin with one article, one essay, or a set of rough sections. Organizational folders can hold material that should remain in the project without appearing in the final export. Later, chapters and subchapters can grow around the material instead of forcing you to design the entire table of contents first.

Use collections as temporary working desks

The structural tree does not need to change every time your focus changes. Manual collections can gather only the sections you are editing this week, while saved searches can surface text by status, type, or query. That is useful for books assembled from interviews, essays, research notes, or previously published articles.

Keep the argument visible

The Idea Map is useful beyond fiction. Place claims, evidence, chapters, notes, images, and real manuscript sections on a board. Connections can show dependencies or relationships between ideas, while groups can later become manuscript structure.

Revision is part of research

Nonfiction often changes when evidence changes. Multiple versions let you preserve an earlier argument, compare revisions, lock a source or approved version, and keep editorial comments attached to the relevant passage.

Prepare the package around the manuscript

The publishing workspace keeps positioning, blurb, synopsis or proposal, author information, rights, and AI-use declaration separate from the manuscript itself. A readiness checklist makes missing publishing material visible before export.

Good fit if…

Your nonfiction project begins as a pile of material and gradually becomes a book, report, essay collection, or long-form manuscript — and you want the research context to stay with the writing instead of being scattered across folders and apps.
