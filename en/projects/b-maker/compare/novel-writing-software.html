Novel writingFiction

Novel writing software should hold the story, not just the words.

A novel becomes harder to manage long before it becomes hard to type. Chapters, scenes, characters, revisions, plot threads, research, and publishing decisions need to stay connected.

B-Maker for novelists

Start with a chapter and a clean editor. Add structure, characters, plotlines, an idea map, manuscript board, versions, analytics, and publishing tools only as the novel grows.

Why a novel stops behaving like one document

A short draft can live in a word processor. A longer novel usually becomes a small system: scenes move, a character changes motivation, a subplot appears in six chapters, and an earlier version suddenly becomes useful again. The friction comes from keeping those pieces synchronized.

One manuscript, several useful views

B-Maker keeps the structural manuscript tree as the source of truth, while offering different working views over it. Use the continuous editor to read the whole manuscript, collections to focus on a subset, the manuscript board to work by status, and the story grid to see plotlines across chapters and scenes.

Plan without creating a second project

Characters, relationships, locations, plotlines, arc points, planning cards, and notes live beside the manuscript. The Idea Map can contain real project entities as well as freeform notes, and those notes can become actual chapters or sections rather than being copied into another tool.

Rewrite without losing yesterday

Keep multiple versions of a chapter or scene, lock finished versions, compare revisions, and attach comments to whole texts or specific passages. The goal is to make experimentation cheap without producing folders full of files named final-2 and final-really-final.

Finish the novel, not just the draft

Analytics and editorial checks surface structural gaps, repetition, long passages, draft markers, and other signals. Then the full-book preview and publishing workspace help move from manuscript to EPUB, PDF, DOCX, TXT, or Markdown.

Good fit if…

You want to begin with a simple writing surface but expect the project to grow into characters, plotlines, versions, visual planning, editorial checks, and publishing preparation — without splitting the novel across several apps.
