B-Maker vs Scrivener

Scrivener is the mature long-form power tool. B-Maker takes a more opinionated local-first approach: start writing quickly, then bring structure, planning, visual thinking, revisions, analytics, and publishing around the same portable project.

B-Maker / Guides & comparisons / B-Maker vs Scrivener
Short answer

Choose Scrivener if you want a proven, extremely deep long-form writing environment and are willing to learn it. Try B-Maker if your priority is a faster entry point plus an integrated idea map, project-aware planning, versions, analytics, and publishing tools in one portable project.

The biggest difference is philosophy

Scrivener has had years to grow into a broad professional environment. Its strength is depth: the Binder, Corkboard, Outliner, metadata, research storage, Snapshots, flexible Compile system, and many other tools can support very complex manuscripts.

B-Maker exposes the simplest path first: create or open a project, add a chapter or article, write. The deeper tools are there when needed — continuous full-manuscript editing, collections and saved searches, project-aware idea boards, story planning, revisions, text checks, analytics, illustrations, and publishing preparation.

Visual thinking and structure

B-Maker now includes an Idea Map inside the project rather than treating visual planning as a separate application. Notes, chapters, scenes, characters, plotlines, images, and planning cards can share multiple boards, and map notes or groups can become real manuscript sections. Scrivener's visual planning centers on Corkboard and Outliner views, with the separate Scapple application available for more freeform mapping.

This is not automatically better: Scrivener's Binder/Outliner/Corkboard model is mature and highly proven. B-Maker's advantage is tighter continuity between freeform visual thinking and the project entities that become the manuscript.

Project ownership and sync

B-Maker currently has no mandatory account or cloud backend. A project is stored as a portable .bmaker file, and the web version can download the browser project as that file. That is useful if you prefer local storage and your own backup or cloud-drive workflow, but it also means there is no built-in cross-device synchronization or real-time collaboration today.

Scrivener is also fundamentally desktop-file oriented, but its iOS workflow supports Dropbox synchronization. Literature & Latte sells macOS, Windows, and iOS versions, with platform licensing handled separately.

Revisions

Both products recognize that writers need to rewrite without destroying previous work. Scrivener provides Snapshots and comparison tools. B-Maker stores multiple versions of chapters, scenes, or articles, supports full-screen comparison, and can lock finished versions into read-only mode.

Export and publishing

Scrivener's Compile system is considerably more mature and configurable. That flexibility is valuable if you need detailed output control. B-Maker takes a more opinionated route: preview the complete book as PDF, control which working sections and headings enter the final book, prepare positioning/blurb/synopsis/author/rights information with a readiness checklist, then export the complete project or a selected branch.

Who should choose Scrivener?

  • You want a mature tool with years of development behind it.
  • You need very deep outlining, metadata, research, and Compile options.
  • You work on macOS and want a native app now.
  • You already have a Scrivener workflow that works.

Who should try B-Maker?

  • You want to start writing before learning a large application.
  • You want freeform idea mapping to stay connected to real chapters, scenes, characters, and plotlines.
  • You like one portable local-first project containing manuscript, planning, versions, analytics, and publishing data.
  • You also keep article series or working archives alongside longer projects.

Frequently asked questions

Is B-Maker a Scrivener clone?

No. The products overlap in long-form writing, but B-Maker is built around progressive complexity, one portable project file, and an integrated Idea Map.

Does B-Maker replace every Scrivener feature?

No. Scrivener is older and deeper in several areas, especially Compile and its mature native ecosystem.

Verdict

Scrivener remains the stronger choice if maximum maturity and configurability matter most. B-Maker is now more than a simplified editor: its case is continuity — article or chapter, idea board, manuscript structure, revision, analysis, and publication preparation can stay in the same project with fewer application boundaries.
