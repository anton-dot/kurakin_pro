Why have both Focus Mode and Split View?

Because sometimes context is noise, and sometimes context is exactly what the task requires.

The difference

Focus Mode reduces visible context. Split View deliberately keeps two contexts available.

Focus Mode is for local work

Finishing a scene, rewriting a difficult paragraph or drafting from scratch often benefits from fewer controls and fewer neighboring structures.

Split View is for relational work

Compare a chapter with notes, keep reference material visible, inspect two nearby sections or revise while looking at another part of the project.

Screenshot: Focus Mode vs Split ViewSame manuscript task shown twice: a clean single writing surface in Focus Mode and two coordinated panes in Split View.assets/img/bmaker/bmaker-explained-focus-split-01.webp

These are opposite tools for opposite moments

A productive editor does not maximize visible information. It lets the writer choose how much context this task needs.
