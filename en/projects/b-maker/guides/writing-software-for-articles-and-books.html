Articles + books

Writing software for articles and books in one project.

You do not have to choose between a blogging archive and a book-writing app. Start with articles, keep published work out of the way, and let a larger manuscript grow from the same material.

The short version

B-Maker can hold articles, essays, research notes, and a book structure in the same portable project. Drafts stay visible, published pieces can move into an organizational folder excluded from book export, and any section can still be searched, revised, versioned, or exported separately.

Most writers do not begin with “Chapter 1”

A book often starts as fragments: an article that worked, three notes around the same idea, an essay, a research file, or a series of posts that slowly reveal a bigger argument. Traditional word processors are fine for each individual file, but the archive becomes harder to manage as the number of pieces grows.

B-Maker treats the collection itself as a project. You can start with one article, add another, and introduce deeper structure only when the material actually needs it.

A simple article workflow

A practical project can begin with folders such as Drafts, Published, Research, and Book draft. Active work remains near the top. When an article is published, move it into the Published folder and collapse that branch. The text remains searchable and editable, but it stops occupying attention every time you open the project.

Organizational folders can be excluded from book export together with their subtree. That means your archive can live beside the future manuscript without accidentally becoming part of the final EPUB or PDF.

Versions matter for articles too

Articles are rarely finished in one pass. B-Maker keeps multiple versions of a section inside the project, so the workflow does not need files named article-final.docx, article-final-2.docx, and article-final-really-final.docx. You can compare versions, lock a finished revision, and return to an older direction later.

From article series to book manuscript

The useful part is that the archive does not have to be rebuilt when the project grows. A series of related articles can become a manual collection, a saved search, or a new branch in the manuscript tree. Notes on the Idea Map can become real chapters. Existing sections can be reordered into a larger structure while the original published pieces stay archived.

This makes B-Maker useful for nonfiction authors, technical writers, consultants, researchers, and anyone whose book is likely to emerge from material written over months or years.

Export one article or the whole book

You can export a selected section with its nested subsections instead of exporting the entire project. So the same project can produce a single DOCX article today and an EPUB book later.

When this approach fits

It fits especially well when you publish regularly, want one searchable archive, reuse research across pieces, or expect a future long-form work to grow from shorter writing. If every article is independent and you never need versions, planning, relationships, or long-term structure, a simple folder of documents may still be enough.

Start with one article.

Do not build a publishing system before you have something to publish. Open B-Maker, write one piece, and let the project become more structured only when your writing does.
