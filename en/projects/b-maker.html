From articles to a book

Start with an article. Grow into a book.

You do not have to begin with a 500-page novel. Write one article. Then another. Find your rhythm, collect ideas, and build up material without committing to a huge structure on day one.

Keep active pieces in Drafts and move finished work to an organizational folder such as Published. The folder and its subtree stay out of the book export, and when you collapse it, completed work stops cluttering the working view while remaining searchable inside the same project.

Over time, a series of articles can become the raw material for a larger work — and when it does, the notes, drafts, versions, and context are already there.

Start writing first. Let the bigger structure appear when you need it.

Write and organize

See one chapter — or the whole manuscript.

Write in a single chapter or open the full-manuscript editor and read chapters and nested sections as one continuous scroll. The text still saves independently in each current version.

Keep the main tree structural, then create manual collections with their own order or saved searches by text, status, and section type. Organize a view without moving the manuscript itself.

Structure when you need it. A clean writing surface when you do not.

Think visually

The idea map is part of the writing project.

Place notes, chapters, scenes, characters, plotlines, images, and planning cards on freeform boards. Connect them with directed or undirected links, search and filter the canvas, use multiple boards, a minimap, automatic layout, stacks, and magnetic areas.

More importantly, the map is not decorative. Drag project entities onto it, jump between the map, editor, and planning screens, add an entire structure branch, or turn map notes and groups into real chapters and sections with an initial text version.

Ideas can become manuscript instead of being copied into it.

Plan the story

For books, the story is more than a list of chapters.

Track characters, custom properties, relationships, arc points by scene, plotlines, locations, and project notes. Link planning cards back to manuscript elements in both directions.

The manuscript board gives you another view of the work: statuses, search, and filters by characters and plotlines without changing the underlying book structure.

Revision

Rewrite without destroying yesterday's work.

Keep several revisions of a chapter, scene, or article inside the project. Rename, duplicate, compare, lock a finished version, choose the current one, and return to an earlier idea when you need it.

Comments can belong to the whole text or a specific passage, and locked versions open read-only so finished work is harder to change by accident.

No more final.docx, final-2.docx, and REAL-final.docx.

Prepare to publish

From draft to a publication-ready package.

Preview the complete book as PDF using the current versions, cover, illustrations, hidden headings, and explicit page breaks. Decide which sections appear in the final book without deleting useful working material.

The publishing workspace separates positioning, blurb, synopsis or proposal, author information, rights, and AI-use declaration. Required fields, typical limits, explanations, and a live readiness checklist help show what is still missing.

Export the whole project or a selected section with its subsections — including a single article stored inside an archive folder.

Control the work

Useful tools stay out of the way until you need them.

Editorial checks

Russian and English checks for typos, mixed alphabets, repetition, punctuation, dialogue typography, long sentences, filler words, and more.

Author-friendly analytics

Words, characters, reading time, author sheets, estimated A4/A5 pages, dialogue share, repetition, and structural issues.

Goals and sessions

Book and chapter goals, daily activity, and writing-session history.

Safe structure

Exclude service sections from export, hide headings, force page breaks, and restore deleted nested sections from Trash.

Import and export

Bring TXT, Markdown, and DOCX into a new or existing project and export a whole book or selected branch.

Backups and integrity

Manual backups, backup history, restore, SQLite integrity check, and pre-export manuscript validation.

Local-first

One project. One file. Yours.

A B-Maker project is a portable .bmaker SQLite file. Your books, articles, versions, planning, comments, cover, and illustrations do not have to live in somebody else's cloud.

Keep it on your computer, back it up however you prefer, move it to another machine, or download the browser project as a portable .bmaker file.

No mandatory cloud storage
No server dependency for your writing
